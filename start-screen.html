A start screen that says pick up your phone

Today the start screen offers five buttons of equal weight, and the phone is the last of them. To get nearly everyone driving with their phone, the start screen should stop being a menu and become one instruction: scan this, and your phone is the steering wheel.

Every screen below is a drawing, not the game. Nothing here is built. The four actions today are: click Phone wheel, scan, tap start on the phone, then go back to the laptop and click START.

What's wrong with today's screen

  • The big button skips the phone. START is the loudest thing on screen and it works immediately, so the easy path is the worst experience.
  • Five choices before one lap. Steering, mode, preview, weather and phone all look the same. A newcomer can't tell which matter.
  • The labels are ours, not theirs. "Steering: KEYBOARD" and "Today: RAIN AT DUSK" describe settings. They don't say what to do.
  • The phone needs a detour. You click a button to open a panel, scan, tap on the phone, then come back to the laptop to press START.

The proposal

Change 1 · the big one

The start screen is the scan screen

The code is on screen the moment the page loads, with no button to press first. The headline says what it is in plain words, and three short steps say what to do. There is no START button yet, because there is nothing to start until a wheel is connected.

  • The steps are numbered because they are a sequence, and the third happens on the phone.
  • The short code stays under the picture for anyone whose camera won't scan.
  • Today's weather becomes a small label. It's information, not a choice.
Change 2

When the phone connects, the screen changes to "ready"

The code disappears and a wheel takes its place, turning live as you tilt the phone. That is the proof it works, before any lap starts. This is also where the one real choice lives: Beginner or Pro, each explained in a few words.

  • Starting from the phone means your hands never leave the wheel.
  • "Preview lap" becomes "Watch a lap first" and moves to the quiet row with the leaderboard and help.
Change 3

The phone shows one big button, laid out sideways

After scanning, the phone asks you to turn it sideways, then shows START and nothing else. In Pro it also shows the brake hint from the earlier mockups: the lamp in the hub and faint thumb pads that fade after your first brake.

Change 4

If someone opens the link on a phone, say so kindly

Plenty of people will tap the link from YouTube or Slack on their phone. Today they get a start screen that makes little sense there. They should get one clear message, and a way to send themselves the link.

Things to decide first

The pitch changes. The film and the trailer both say "steered with your bare hands", and the trailer's end card says "One lap. Steered with your bare hands." If nearly everyone drives with a phone, the headline on the game says something different from the videos. Either the videos gain a line about the phone, or hands stay as a visible second choice rather than a footnote.

  • Which mode is selected by default? I'd make it Beginner for a first visit and remember the choice after that. You haven't decided this yet.
  • Can the phone start the lap? The mockups assume yes. It needs the phone to send a "go" to the game, which the link can already carry.
  • Is a code made on every visit? Showing the code on load means every visitor creates one, scanned or not. Codes are small and expire, but it is more traffic to the pairing service than today.
  • What if the phone can't connect? Some office and guest networks block the direct link between phone and laptop. After a few seconds the screen should say so and offer hands or keyboard, rather than sit on "connecting".

How we'd know it worked

Every lap on the leaderboard is already tagged as phone, hands or keys. So the share of laps driven by phone is a number we can read today, and read again after the change. "99%" is your target; I'd treat anything above nine in ten as the screen doing its job, since some people will never have a phone to hand.
