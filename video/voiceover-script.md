# This Little Thing: Golden Lap voiceover

Third draft: recut around the bet. The question that runs through the film is "can I beat the machine's one-minute lap with my bare hands?", with the times falling on screen. The steering fault is now a late complication, not the hook. Every number is from LOG.md. Eleven scenes; each is one take. Lines starting with ">" are directions, not words to say.

## 1. Cold open: the bet

> Two or three seconds of engine and "tape" before the first word.

That isn't footage. There's no car. No track. No engine. I typed it. This is a racing game, and I steer it with my bare hands. No wheel. No controller. Just a webcam, and me, holding nothing. I built it over a weekend with Claude, without writing a line of code. Now, the computer that built it can drive a lap in one minute. So here's the bet. Can I beat it, with my hands? Stay to the end, and I'll give you the link, so you can beat me.

## 2. Setup

To explain, we go back to the start of the weekend. I'd been watching old Formula One onboard footage. The camera sits on the driver's shoulder. You see the helmet, the front tyre, and two hands sawing at the wheel. And the picture is soft, and washed out, like a tape that's been played too many times. So I opened Claude, on the Fable five point two model, and asked one question. Could we make a racing game, in a web page, that looks like this?

## 3. How the look is made

Its answer surprised me. It said most of that look isn't the car, and it isn't the track. It's the camera, and the tape. So it built a plain little world. Flat colours. Trees like folded paper. Which is, frankly, rubbish. Then it ran the picture through a filter. Bend the lens. Smear the colour sideways. Blow out the sky. And the same frame turns into footage. Twenty minutes in, I had a car, a track, and a lap.

## 4. Looking

Then I started looking. And that's the whole job, really. You look, and you say what's wrong. The front wheel was hovering inside the barrier. There was a black slab where a shadow should be. The road had no tyre marks, so there was no feeling of speed. One thing at a time, it fixed them. The driver's head even bobs about in the mirror. The one thing I never asked it to change was the helmet. The helmet was perfect. Then sound, which matters more than you'd think.

> The engine comes up to full volume here. Shout the next four lines over it, properly, as if standing next to the car.

That engine isn't a recording of a real car! I described it, in words! And ElevenLabs made it! Five loops, blended together, as the revs climb!

> The engine cuts dead. Normal voice, slightly sheepish.

Sorry.

## 5. The circuit, and the time to beat

The track is a greatest hits. The best corners, from the best circuits, joined into one lap. A swoop like Spa. A long right like Monza. The tunnel, from Monaco. And to test it, Claude gave the game a driver of its own. It never misses a braking point. And it laps in one minute. That's the time to beat.

## 6. How the track is made

So how do you make a race track? It starts with dots. Sixty of them. Then a smooth curve through every dot, which is the racing line down the middle of the road. Chop that curve up, three metres at a time, and at each step, work out which way is forward, and which way is right. Go six metres to each side, join it all up with triangles, and that's tarmac. Where the curve is tight, it paints a kerb. And every dot has a height, so the road climbs, and falls. That's the whole circuit. Four kilometres, from sixty dots.

## 7. The problem

So I had a go. My first lap was one minute forty-four. On a keyboard, the steering was a mess. Tap a key, and the car twitched. We tried four times to fix it, and I got down to one minute six. Still six seconds short. Then I had a slightly silly idea. I'd seen music apps you play by waving at the camera. So what if I just held my hands up, like a wheel?

## 8. Hands

So that's what it does. The camera finds my two hands. The tilt of the line between them is the steering. Squeeze them together, to brake. And because it can see my face, it puts me in the helmet, in the car, on the track. And I got faster. A lot faster. One minute, point three four. A third of a second over.

## 9. The win

> Leave a real pause before the time.

Two corners before the tunnel were slowing the whole lap down. So we opened them up. On the new lap, the computer's own driver went round in one minute flat. And me? Fifty-three point five seconds. With my hands. Nearly seven seconds quicker than the machine.

## 10. The turn

There was one more problem. After about five laps, the steering died. The car just carried on, without me. Claude's first theory was my laptop overheating. Wrong. Its second was my arms getting tired, and dropping out of shot. Also wrong. And it couldn't see the problem for itself, because it has no camera, and no hands. So it built a black box. A little panel that records everything the steering does. I drove until it broke again, and sent back one screenshot. And there it was. Every two minutes, the hand tracker reports a hand with no fingers. The code asked where the knuckle was, got nothing, and fell over. It had been there since the very first day.

## 11. Payoff

With that fixed, we kept going. We added the corners Formula One drivers say they love most. Rain. Fog. You can even use your phone as the steering wheel. More than fifty versions, in one weekend. None of it needed me to write code. It needed an eye, and the patience to keep saying, not quite. The lap is longer now, and the leaderboard has started again. So first place, is anyone's. The link's below. Have a drive. And beat me, if you can.
