Can I build a pro F1 game in one day?

Runtime 8:02Panels 20Black-box sections 7Key decisions 12Day one Tue 17:05 → Wed 17:05

The story is the clock. It opens on the trailer, asks the one-day question with 24 hours on screen, and answers it when the clock runs out. Halfway through, a second question takes over: can I beat the computer's lap with my bare hands? In between, seven times, we open the lid and show how Claude actually made it: the wire underneath, a circuit from sixty dots, a forest from one lumpy ball. That's the gold, the way it was in the Golden Hour Ride film.

Rules for the edit

Lean into the action

Every explanation plays over driving at full speed, never over a static slide. No more than about 30 seconds without a shot flat out with the engine up.

The clock never leaves

From panel 2 to panel 17, a 24-hour countdown sits top right. Real times from the log appear as captions: 17:25 first frame, 17:39 online, 06:02 hands, 13:12 the one-minute lap.

Open the black box

Seven short sections show how it works inside, each in plain steps with real numbers. Same graphic each time: the frame drops into a box and the lid lifts.

Name the decisions

Each key decision gets a numbered marker on screen the moment it is made, so the viewer sees the choices, not just the result.

07Why is it so watchable?

Picture
Freeze the tunnel frame. Draw the golden lines on: the horizon sits on the top line, the vanishing point on the top-right crossing. Then the three side-by-sides: eye at 0.95 m vs 1.70 m, lens 92° vs 60°, blur on vs off. End on the five camera numbers typed out.
Voice
So why is it so watchable? I assumed some maths, the golden ratio maybe. It wasn't. The whole camera is five numbers, tuned by eye. And yet, look. The horizon lands on the golden line. The tunnel disappears right on the golden point. Raise the camera to head height, and the speed goes. Narrow the lens, and the walls slow down. Turn the blur off, and it's a video game again. It didn't use a formula. It copied the spot where teams in the nineties bolted their cameras, the one place on a car where speed looks terrifying.
Sound
Each comparison plays with its own engine sound.

Key decisions

The choices that made it work, with when they happened. Each one appears in the film where it is made.

  1. 1
    TUE 17:10
    One web page, not a game engine

    Three.js in a single file. No downloads, no build step; anyone can open the link and play.

  2. 2
    TUE 17:06
    The look is the camera and the tape, not the graphics

    "About 70% of the look is one shader." Low-poly trees stop mattering once the tape is on.

  3. 3
    TUE 17:20
    Tune the camera by eye against the reference

    Lowered, levelled, 80° to 92° lens, axle 0.5 m closer. Five numbers, no formula.

  4. 4
    TUE 17:30
    An original lap built from the best corners, about a minute long

    Borrows corner shapes, copies no real track. A minute means one more go.

  5. 5
    TUE 17:39
    Online straight away, one shared leaderboard

    Published by Spacefast 34 minutes in. A board turns a demo into a game.

  6. 6
    TUE 18:45
    A described engine over a synthesised one

    ElevenLabs loops, measured by pitch, cross-faded with the revs. "The sound is incredibly important."

  7. 7
    WED 06:02
    Steer with your hands

    Real analogue steering from a webcam, which the keyboard could never give.

  8. 8
    THU
    Squeeze to brake, full throttle at rest

    From the first user test. How you would grip a real wheel.

  9. 9
    WED
    Beginner and Pro

    The car picks the speed in Beginner, so a newcomer only steers.

  10. 10
    THU
    Copy what already worked

    The rain and the road surface came from the cycling game.

  11. 11
    THU
    The phone becomes the main wheel

    Haptics, tilt to steer, and start or go again from across the room.

  12. 12
    SAT
    Cut what can't be read at speed

    The Spacefast banner: lovely in a still, a blur at 280 km/h.
